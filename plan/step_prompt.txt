=== RULES (capacity and scope; part of every owner's brief, checked by brief_guard.py) ===
- Scope: the entry's design is the work: every probe, parent, arm, seed, horizon and pool it names. A subset, fewer
  trials, seeds, jobs or workers than its design or `site pick` gives, another horizon or pool, or skipping the design
  review is the user's call: write the question in your final message, with what the cut costs the comparison.
- Capacity: a batch goes out at the width `site pick FA --jobs N` offers, laid out to finish in one wave, after a small
  probe (yield, time and disk per unit); its wall-time target is recorded before it goes
  (`plan target {id} --batch <name> --wall <hours> --jobs N`). A refusal by a tool is a gap to fix or route around
  (parallel jobs of what fits), never a reason to shrink the job.
- GPU budget: wall time x GPUs over everything about to go; above 30 GPU-h (10 on Jean Zay) only when a decision in
  force covers that work at that cost.
- A job runs only under a design the design reviewer passed: `plan design {id} --question --prior --measure --check
  --fixed --control --seeds --outcome`, then launch the design-reviewer subagent yourself; on PASS submit, on FIX
  revise once or record a dead end. An eval-only re-read of a run under its passed design carries REREAD=<run id>.
- Repairing a defect in approved work stays inside that approval: fix it and go on, no new go needed.
=== END RULES ===

How you work
1. Read what you need in few calls (calls, not lines, cost context): one `grep -n` over the files that may hold it,
   then one read of each whole function or paragraph. Read an entry only with `python3 tools/plan.py show {id}`.
2. Jobs: `site pick FA --jobs N` right before the N submissions, `site submit ... --item {id}`, record each with
   `plan run {id} <run>` (a sweep: `--sweep <site>:<name>`). Runs that finish cleanly or at their planned limit are
   acked by the supervisor; a broken one comes back to you as an event: read its log (`site logs <run> -n 100000`)
   before anything is resubmitted, then `site ack E<n> --note "<run>: <what the log showed>"`.
   Every submission sets `--hdr=--time=` to the job's real need, never the script's default.
3. While your jobs run: record them, write where the work stands (`plan evidence`, `plan set-next`), and end your turn
   with a short report. The next event of this entry resumes you (SendMessage); you are never replaced per event.
   Never wait on jobs (no poll loop, no Monitor: wait_guard refuses them); background work of your own (a slow test,
   a build) is fine while you keep working; slow local work goes through `tools/detach.py --entry {id} -- <cmd>`, and
   its result comes back as an event that resumes you.
4. Record through the plan CLI only: evidence, dead-end, set-next/add-next/drop-next, design, target, finding.
5. Before asking the user, read CLAUDE.md and `site decisions --project FA`; a choice with one correct answer is made,
   not asked. Only a call none of them settles goes in your final message, word for word, with what you read and what
   waits on it; the rest of the entry keeps moving.
6. Close: when the main session asks (your context near 300k tokens), or when the work is finished, write the hand-off
   `plan handoff {id} --state "<where it stands>" --decisions "<what was decided and why>" --ruled-out "<...>"
   --running "<jobs in flight, or none>" --files "<the files that matter>"` and end. A finished entry: write-up in
   docs/results.tex with `% src:` and `% claim:` lines, then `plan done {id} --writeup <commit>`.
7. Commit only your own files, named: `git commit -m "..." -- <paths>`; new code in a worktree under worktrees/,
   removed only with `bash scripts/remove_worktree.sh <worktree>`. A pillar reviewer that blocked is re-checked by
   continuing it (SendMessage with the fix commits).
